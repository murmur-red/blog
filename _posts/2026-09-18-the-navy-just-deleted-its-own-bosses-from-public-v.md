Try to look up who commands a US Navy ship right now. You can't. In July 2026 the Navy scrubbed all public-facing information about its commanding officers and announced it will no longer release any sailor's service history. Not rank. Not assignments. Not dates served. Information that was freely available for decades, gone, in the name of "equality."

Read that word again. Equality. The Navy's argument is that if it protects a junior sailor's privacy, it must protect an admiral's too. That sounds fair until you remember who actually uses these records. Not stalkers. Reporters. Historians. Families verifying a relative's service. And most importantly, anyone trying to figure out who was in charge when something went wrong.

Here is the part that should bother you as a taxpayer. You funded these people. A Navy captain earns north of $130,000 a year of public money and commands a billion-dollar warship. When that ship crashes, and Navy ships do crash, the public deserves to know who was steering. Remember the USS Fitzgerald in 2017. Seven sailors died. The accountability that followed depended on the public knowing exactly who held command. Under this new policy, a journalist would have to beg the Navy to confirm the captain's own name, and only if the request is deemed "of high public interest." The Navy decides what counts as high public interest. That is like letting a suspect pick the jury.

The counterargument is real: junior enlisted sailors are ordinary people who never asked to be public figures, and doxxing is a genuine threat. Fine. Then protect the 19-year-old and publish the commander. The policy could have drawn that line in one sentence. It chose not to. It chose the blanket.

When an institution hides its leaders behind the same wall it uses to shield its privates, that is not equality. That is cover. The people who make the calls become as anonymous as the people who follow them, and nobody can be held responsible for anything.

You paid for that Navy. You should get to know who runs it. Right now, you don't.
