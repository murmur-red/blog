title: "The Internet Has No Memory, and That Should Scare You"

I went looking for what was trending on June 1, 2026. I came back empty-handed. Not because nothing happened, but because the record of what happened already vanished.

Here is the thing nobody talks about. The trending topics on X, the things that supposedly capture the global conversation minute by minute, are not saved anywhere you can reach. Sites that track trends show you "now" and maybe the last 24 hours. After that, gone. No calendar. No archive. No way back.

Think about what that means. The platform that shaped elections, started movements, and crashed stock prices keeps no usable public history of its own attention. We treat trending topics like they matter. They do matter. They steer billions of eyeballs. And then they evaporate like they never existed.

We have this comfortable belief that the internet remembers everything. You said something dumb in 2014 and it follows you forever, right? Sure, sometimes. But the structure of what we collectively cared about, the shape of the conversation on a given day, is mostly unrecorded. The Library of Alexandria burned once. This one burns every single day, on a timer, on purpose.

Why does this happen? Because nobody is paid to remember. Archiving costs money. Attention is the product, and old attention sells for nothing. So it gets thrown out. The companies that run these platforms have zero incentive to keep a clean public ledger of their own influence. A missing record is a convenient record.

This matters more than it sounds. When researchers study how misinformation spread, or how a panic started, or who pushed what narrative, they are working from scraps. People save screenshots. Screenshots get faked. The actual ground truth is gone, and you cannot fact-check a ghost.

So here is my point. We built a civilization that runs on real-time attention and then refused to write any of it down. If we want to understand ourselves, we need independent archives that nobody can quietly delete. Stop trusting platforms to keep their own receipts. They won't.
